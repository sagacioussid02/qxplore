1 / 3  Build a circuit with the Hadamard gate

The Hadamard gate H is the mixer: it turns a certain qubit into an even 50/50 mix. H|0⟩ = |+⟩, H|1⟩ = |−⟩ — both measure 50/50, but the minus sign in |−⟩ is a real difference the next gate can feel. Pick a starting state, then apply H as many times as you like.

2 / 3  Inspect the state — what the simulator sees

Before you measure, you can ask a simulator to print the whole state vector — every amplitude, including the minus sign a measurement would hide. That is how you check a circuit: the printout shows the mix itself, not just one outcome.

3 / 3  Measure it — many times

One measurement gives one outcome, not the mix. To see the 50/50, you have to prepare the same state and measure it over and over. Each click below re-prepares your circuit from scratch, then measures once.

No measurements yet — build a circuit, then measure it.

Worked example: start in |0⟩ and apply H once — you get |+⟩, a 50/50 mix, and measuring 100 times lands near 50 zeros and 50 ones. Apply H again and the mix undoes itself: you are back at |0⟩, and every measurement gives 0. Two Hadamards cancel — a superposition is a reversible turn, not noise. The same happens from |1⟩: H gives |−⟩, and a second H returns |1⟩.
