1 / 4  Pick a two-qubit state

Two qubits can be linked so that the pair has a state neither qubit has on its own — that is entanglement. The four Bell states are the simplest entangled pairs: Φ⁺ and Φ⁻ always agree when measured, Ψ⁺ and Ψ⁻ always disagree. The worked example is Φ⁺, and the last preset is an independent pair for comparison — no link at all.

|ψ⟩ = 0.71|00⟩ + 0.71|11⟩

Can this state be split into two single-qubit states? The test: a product (a|0⟩ + b|1⟩) ⊗ (c|0⟩ + d|1⟩) needs a₀₀ · a₁₁ to equal a₀₁ · a₁₀. Here 0.71 · 0.71 = 0.50, but 0 · 0 = 0.00 — they differ, so no two single-qubit states multiply to this one. Entangled.

Each qubit on its own: the first is 50% 0 / 50% 1, the second is 50% 0 / 50% 1 — perfectly random alone, yet together they agree 100% of the time.

2 / 4  Build it: Hadamard, then CNOT

An entangled pair is made with two gates. A Hadamard on the first qubit mixes it; then a CNOT — a controlled-NOT — flips the second qubit only where the first is 1. The Hadamard alone leaves the qubits independent; it is the CNOT that links them. Step through the build for the state you picked.

3 / 4  Measure the whole register

Measuring the register reads out both bits at once. For Φ⁺ only 00 and 11 ever appear — 01 and 10 never do. One measurement gives one bitstring; measure many fresh pairs to see the pattern.

4 / 4  Measure just one qubit

Now read only one qubit of the pair. For an entangled pair, that single reading decides the other qubit too — measure the first and see 1, and the second is certainly 1, even though it was 50/50 a moment ago. For the independent pair, measuring one qubit leaves the other exactly as it was. Try both and compare.

No single-qubit measurement yet — pick a qubit above and press Measure this qubit once.

Worked example: prepare Φ⁺ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩) / √2 and measure the register 100 times — you land near 50 × 00 and 50 × 11, and never see 01 or 10. Now take a fresh pair and measure only the first qubit: half the time you see 0 and the second qubit becomes certainly |0⟩; half the time you see 1 and it becomes certainly |1⟩. Compare with the independent pair |+⟩ ⊗ |0⟩: measuring its first qubit never changes the second, because the two still have states of their own.
