1 / 3  Pick a gate — every gate is a reversible turn

A gate rotates the qubit on the Bloch sphere. X flips the bit (|0⟩ ↔ |1⟩), Z flips the phase (Z|1⟩ = −|1⟩), Y does both, H mixes, S is a quarter-turn of phase and T an eighth-turn. Rz turns by any angle you choose. Every gate has an inverse that undoes it — try Undo and watch the state come back.

Amplitudes can be complex — the i in S and T is a real part of the state, not a decoration. θ sets the probabilities (P(0) = cos²(θ/2)); φ is the phase, which a plain measurement cannot see but the next gate can.

3 / 3  Measure it — many times

One measurement gives one outcome, not the mix. To see the probabilities, prepare the same state and measure it over and over. Each click below re-prepares your circuit from scratch, then measures once.

No measurements yet — build a circuit, then measure it.

Worked example: start in |0⟩ and apply H, Z, H. H makes |+⟩, Z flips the phase to |−⟩, and the second H turns |−⟩ into |1⟩ — so H–Z–H turns |0⟩ into |1⟩. Leave the Z out and the two Hadamards cancel, leaving |0⟩. The Z changed nothing a direct measurement could see, yet it flipped the final answer. Also try S, S from |+⟩: two quarter-turns make a Z, so |+⟩ becomes |−⟩.
