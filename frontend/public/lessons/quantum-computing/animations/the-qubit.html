The two numbers are the amplitudes. Square them and you get the probabilities — and the probabilities always add to 1.

Measuring collapses the mix: the qubit becomes whichever outcome you saw, and the other possibility is gone. Measure again right away and you get the same answer every time.

One measurement tells you almost nothing — the probabilities only show up over many runs.
