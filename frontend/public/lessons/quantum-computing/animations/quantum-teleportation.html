3 / 4  Teleport it — measure, send two bits, correct

Alice measures her two qubits and gets two ordinary classical bits — each of the four outcomes is equally likely, 25%. She sends the bits to Bob, who applies the correction from the table. After the correction, Bob's qubit is exactly |ψ⟩ — and Alice's original is gone: her measurement destroyed it.

4 / 4  What if the two bits never arrive?

Before the classical bits reach him, Bob's half of the pair holds nothing about |ψ⟩ at all — the entanglement alone carries no message, which is why nothing travels faster than light. Check it for the state you picked:

Press the button to look at Bob's qubit before any classical bits have arrived.

Worked example: teleport |ψ⟩ = (3/5)|0⟩ + (4/5)|1⟩. Each of Alice's four outcomes has probability 1/4. Suppose she measures 01: Bob holds (4/5)|0⟩ + (3/5)|1⟩ — the amplitudes are swapped — so he applies X, swapping them back, and his qubit is exactly |ψ⟩. Alice's measurement collapsed her qubits to the bits 01, so her copy of |ψ⟩ is gone: the state moved, it was not copied.
